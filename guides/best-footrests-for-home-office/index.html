Best Footrest for Shorter Users at Fixed Desks

Measure the unsupported foot gap after setting chair height for the desk, then compare lift range, under-desk footprint, adjustment, and floor stability.

Quick verdict

Kensington SmartFit SoleMate Plus K52789WW is the best-researched current default for a shorter user whose chair must sit higher to match a fixed desk. Its documented adjustment range covers roughly 3.5 to 5 inches, and its 6.6-pound, rubber-bottomed platform is a credible stability advantage when the desk bay can fit it.

It is not a universal comfort fix. Measure the vertical gap and the clear floor area before buying. Measure before choosing a footrest

  1. Set chair height for the work surface: With shoulders relaxed, position the chair so the elbows can reach the keyboard without shrugging. Do not lower the chair solely to reach the floor if that makes the desktop too high.
  2. Measure the missing support: In that working position, measure from the soles of the shoes you normally wear to the floor. The footrest’s documented range must cover that gap without forcing the knees upward.
  3. Mark the floor footprint: Tape a 21.9-by-14.2-inch rectangle below the desk for the Kensington. Confirm that both feet, the chair base, and the normal pullback path can coexist.
  4. Check the adjustment routine: Decide whether you need to change height during the day and whether a foot-operated control is worth the larger platform.
  5. Test the full setup: Recheck keyboard reach, knee position, chair pullback, and cable clearance together. A footrest solves only the unsupported-feet part of the workstation.

When a footrest is the wrong fix

  • The chair can already sit low enough for supported feet and relaxed keyboard reach.
  • The seat is too deep, the armrests block the desk, or the keyboard remains too far away.
  • The laptop screen is low but direct typing is the real constraint; fix screen and input-device geometry together.
  • The fixed desk is so high that the required chair raise and footrest lift fall outside documented product ranges.
